  SENSITIVITY TO GAMMA, THE ELASTICITY OF SUBSTITUTION BETWEEN NEW AND USED

  The paper sets gamma = 2.0. Edgerton (2011) estimates the elasticity
  between 1.9 and 2.4 for construction machinery; this sweep spans a wider
  range, 1.7 to 2.5. The calibration is held fixed at the benchmark point
  and only prices are re-cleared, so the exercise asks whether the results
  turn on gamma, not whether the model can be re-fitted around it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The results do not turn on gamma. Across 1.7 to 2.5 the misallocation loss
  moves by 0.057 log points, about 1 percent of its level, and the output
  gain by 0.014 points.

  DIRECTION. L FALLS as gamma rises (5.698 at 2.5 against 5.754 at 1.7).
  A claim that a more substitutable pair raises the reported effect, and
  that gamma = 2.0 is therefore the conservative end of the range, is not
  supported: the sign runs the other way. Given the size of the movement
  the honest statement is insensitivity, not a reversal.

  READ THE TWO SWEPT VALUES AGAINST EACH OTHER, NOT AGAINST THE STORED
  BENCHMARK LEVEL. Both gammas give an output gain about 0.24 points below
  the stored gamma = 2.0 figure, and by almost the same amount. A shift
  common to both cannot be a gamma effect; it is a level difference against
  an economy solved in a separate run. The gamma effect is the difference
  BETWEEN 1.7 and 2.5: 0.014 points on the output gain and 0.057 log points
  on L. The L comparison straddles the benchmark (+0.032 and -0.024), so it
  carries no such offset.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW THE ECONOMIES WERE CLEARED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The friction economies clear to 0.2 percent on the goods and labour
  markets and 0.5 percent on the new-to-used ratio, against defaults of 0.5
  and 2 percent. The tighter setting is necessary rather than cosmetic: at
  the default, gamma = 1.7 seeded at the benchmark prices left residuals of
  -0.4 and -1.0 percent, both inside tolerance, so the clearer returned the
  seed unchanged and the economy was never re-cleared. The 2 percent floor
  on the ratio was hardcoded and unreachable from any setting; it is now
  overridable, with the default unchanged.

  The benchmark's own realized gaps are -0.1, +0.1 and +0.3 percent, so it
  already meets the tighter standard and was not re-cleared.

  The frictionless twins clear to 0.5 percent, which is the tolerance
  nofriction.m used for the benchmark's own twin, so the comparison is
  consistent across all three economies.

  gamma is hardcoded inside evaluate_economy and reachable only through
  cf_gamma_override. If that override stopped biting, every gamma would
  solve the same economy and the sweep would report, wrongly, that gamma
  does not matter. The two economies here differ (Y by 1.51e-03), so the
  override is live and the sweep is meaningful.

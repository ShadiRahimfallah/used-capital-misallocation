Method note for the paper:
  Firm-level equipment shares (q30aa/q30ab, self-constructed excluded) are
  aggregated using each firm's machinery and equipment stock at market
  prices, so the statistic is the economy-wide new-to-used capital ratio
  rather than the mean across firms. The distinction matters because larger
  firms hold most of the equipment and are more likely to buy used, so the
  unweighted mean of firm shares overstates Kn/Ku.

TOP-10 PERCENT PAID-EMPLOYMENT SHARE  --  Vietnam SME survey, firm data
==============================================================================

MOMENT
    The share of paid employment held by the largest 10 percent of firms.
    Sort firms by paid employment in descending order, take the top 10 percent
    of firms by count, and report their employment as a share of the total.
    The marginal firm is split fractionally.  This is the data analogue of the
    model estimator in ergodic.m:417-428, which sorts the entrepreneur measure
    by labour demand L and takes the top decile by count.

EMPLOYMENT IS PAID LABOUR
    The model's L_e comes from the firm's labour first-order condition, so
    neither the entrepreneur nor unpaid family workers appear in it.  The
    survey reports the split directly, so no assumption is needed:

        paid employment = total labour force x (1 - share unpaid)

    Unpaid labour is the owner plus family workers.  The 2005 round reports
    unpaid labour as a headcount rather than a share, so paid employment is
    formed by subtraction in that round.

ALL OPERATING FIRMS ARE RETAINED
    Around a fifth of firms report an unpaid share of 1.0: they operate
    entirely on owner and family labour and hire nobody.  They are retained,
    with zero paid employment.  They are operating firms, and the model
    estimator runs over every entrepreneur, each of whom hires L > 0.
    Excluding them would change the population rather than the measure.

SOURCE
    The raw round files enterprise_data/enterprise <year>.dta.  The merged
    vsme_panel.dta carries no paid/unpaid split and cannot produce this
    moment.  Question numbering shifts between rounds, so the variables are
    mapped per round in SPEC above.  The SME Employee files are a sampled
    worker module, not a headcount census, and are reserved for the wage
    Gini moment.

  Check on the choice of year. The 2012-2016 mean real deposit rate is
  3.25 percent, against 3.10 in 2013, so the target does not depend on

  top-decile employment share and the new-to-used ratio are all 2013 SME

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY THE DEPOSIT RATE AND NOT THE LENDING RATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  real deposit  2013: 3.10 percent    2012-2016 mean 3.25
  real lending  2013: 6.09 percent    2012-2016 mean 5.79
  gap           2013: 2.99 points

  Both are correct, and the gap IS Vietnamese financial
  underdevelopment: the return to capital sits above the US benchmark
  of roughly 4.5 percent because capital is scarce, while the household
  savings return sits below it because of repression and a wide
  intermediation spread.

  The model has ONE interest rate. Savers earn r, borrowers pay r, and
  the friction is a quantity constraint -- the collateral limit theta --
  not a price wedge. beta is identified by the household Euler equation,
  so it is pinned by what savers earn, and the deposit rate is the right
  counterpart. Do NOT average the two: the average corresponds to
  nothing in the model. Report robustness over r in [0.03, 0.06]
  instead, which spans the savings return to the borrowing cost.

  METHOD vs NUMBER. Calibrating beta to match the real interest rate,
  rather than assigning beta, follows Tan (2025). The number is the
  Vietnamese series above.

  Viet Nam (CIEM, ILSSA, UCPH and UNU-WIDER), waves 2005 to 2015, pooled.
  The used share is the respondent's own percentage, censored to the
  unit interval. Table 1 is Figure 1 in numbers.

  the self-reported year moves between waves for about a third of
  firms. Age 10 belongs to the 6-10 group, so the top group is 11+.

  is a statement about firms. Table 2 leads with the asset-weighted
  mean because the model's new-to-used capital ratio is a ratio of

  these firms are micro but hold about a fifth of the capital. Each
  table gives the other weighting as a memo.

  percentile of firms holds 29 percent of assets and the top decile 67

  N suggests. That is why the medium+ interval is wide enough to
  contain the micro point estimate: the asset-weighted size ordering
  runs in the direction the model predicts, but no pairwise difference
  between size classes is significant. Report the ordering, not an
  estimated gradient.

  Firm-weighted standard errors are unweighted and are not clustered by
  firm; the within-firm regressions reported separately cluster.

--- 5. HOW TO READ THIS ------------------------------------------------
  Column (1) is BETWEEN firms.  It compares different firms in the same
  sector, year and province, so it licenses only:
      "Older firms report lower used-capital shares than younger firms."

  Column (2) is WITHIN firms.  The same firm is followed across waves and
  alpha_i absorbs everything permanent about it (founder, location, sector,
  technology, its habitual way of answering the question).  A within-firm
  sentence -- "firms reduce their used-capital share as they age" -- has to be
  earned HERE, and only for the margins that actually come out significant.

  All coefficients below are in PERCENTAGE POINTS of the used-machinery share.

  Young, within firm:           +0.76 pp (se 1.66, p = 0.649)
  Young, between firms:         +0.99 pp
  ZeroDebt x Young, within:     +3.88 pp (se 2.34, p = 0.097)
  ZeroDebt x Young, between:    +2.86 pp
  ZeroDebt main effect, within: -1.05 pp (se 0.87, p = 0.230)
  Young + ZeroDebt x Young:     +4.64 pp (se 2.10, p = 0.027)

  VERDICT, one margin at a time:
   * Unconditional age.  Within firm, Young alone is INSIGNIFICANT (p = 0.649) and the
     three-bin age profile is jointly INSIGNIFICANT.  The unconditional sentence
     "firms reduce their used-capital share as they age" is therefore
     NOT supported by the within-firm evidence and should NOT be written.
   * Age interacted with financing.  For DEBT-FREE firms the within-firm young
     premium is 4.6 pp (p = 0.027), and 5.7 pp (p = 0.009) among firms seen in
     at least three waves.  THIS is the sentence the panel supports:
     "within the same firm, the used-capital share is higher in the years when
      the firm is both young and carrying no debt, and falls as it ages out of
      that state."
   * So the referee's correction stands, and it lands on the AGE claim, not on
     the financing claim -- which is the claim the model actually needs.
